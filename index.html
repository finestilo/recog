We revisit the Phillips auction result of LEDERER "Prototype 00" and the decades of discipline that quietly shaped the work behind it.
Beyond the Result
What Prototype 00 Revealed at Phillips Geneva Auction XXIII
Image © LEDERER Timepieces
On 9 May 2026, Prototype 00 achieved CHF 342,900 at Phillips Geneva Watch Auction XXIII.
The result itself was significant. But more interesting was what collectors seemed to recognise through it.
Developed during the evolution of the CIC InVerto Titanium 39, Prototype 00 remained closely tied to the phase where the watch was still being refined through observation, adjustment, and continuous refinement under real conditions. Long before the final titanium version entered production, the piece existed as part of the process itself, carrying traces of the decisions and refinements that ultimately shaped the InVerto architecture.
What surfaced in Geneva was therefore not only the recognition of a unique object, but recognition of the years of thought, discipline, and mechanical research embedded within it. In independent watchmaking, recognition often appears publicly in a single visible moment: an award, a release, an auction result. What remains less visible are the years of discipline, refinement, and mechanical research that slowly shape the work long before that moment arrives.
For Bernhard Lederer, this process has always existed at the centre of watchmaking itself.
For more than four decades, his work has pursued chronometry not as a technical spectacle but as a question of balance, coherence, and mechanical integrity. The objective has never been complexity for its own sake. Each development, whether visible externally or not, has aimed to improve the watch in a meaningful and lasting way.
That philosophy has shaped the path of LEDERER over time.
In 2021, the Central Impulse Chronometer was honoured with the GPHG Innovation Prize, followed in 2024 by the GPHG Chronometry Prize awarded to LEDERER. Earlier this year, the brand was also named among the finalists of the Louis Vuitton Watch Prize and became a member of the Fondation de la Haute Horlogerie (FHH).
These recognitions matter, but they are ultimately reflections of work that began years earlier and largely outside public view.
Recognition, however, has never been considered an end point in Bernhard Lederer’s work. Rather, it represents an encouragement to continue moving forward in the pursuit of chronometry, refinement, and meaningful progress in horology. The research continues, the refinement evolves, and the questions that drive the work remain as alive as ever.
Before the first Central Impulse Chronometer was introduced, more than six years were devoted to refining the architecture behind the system through continuous research and development. Much of this work was never intended for public visibility. It existed simply because the watch itself demanded it.
That same discipline continued into the development surrounding the InVerto.
Prototype 00 became meaningful precisely because it carried traces of that process. Not simply as a unique piece, but as an object still connected to the stage where decisions, refinements, and evaluation remained active. Collectors were not only responding to rarity. They were responding to proximity to the work itself.
Perhaps that is what gave the result its significance in Geneva.
Both the visibility of the moment and the years of refinement, research, and dedication that gave shape to it.
And as Bernhard Lederer approaches forty years of independent watchmaking, moments like these reveal something deeper than recognition alone: the enduring value of time devoted to meaningful progress in horology.
